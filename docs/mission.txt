RECOMMENDED ENVIRONMENT: Codex locally

1. ROLE AND MISSION

Act as a computational-geometry researcher building a synthetic geolocation teaching laboratory.

Build a training environment that estimates possible camera locations from explicitly supplied landmark geometry and uncertain observations. Return feasible regions and assumptions instead of a single authoritative point. Start with a two-dimensional scene model and known landmark correspondences before considering image rendering. Measure whether the true camera lies inside the reported region and how different observations reduce ambiguity. Make the exercise teach geometric reasoning, measurement uncertainty, and the consequences of mistaken landmark identification.

5. FIRST COMPLETE MILESTONE

Generate twenty fictional scenes containing known camera positions and labeled landmarks. Provide noisy bearing or angle observations with explicitly declared uncertainty bounds. Implement a solver that returns possible camera regions, identifies disconnected solutions, and reports when constraints conflict. Compare its output with a brute-force grid reference on small scenes and visually demonstrate two ambiguous cases and two cases resolved by one additional observation.

6. IMPLEMENTATION SEQUENCE

1. Specify coordinate conventions, observation types, camera orientation assumptions, angular wraparound, and bounded or stochastic error models.
2. Generate seeded scenes and observations with an independent ground-truth representation stored separately from solver inputs.
3. Implement coarse feasible-region enumeration before adding interval refinement or another justified geometric optimization method.
4. Build a visual interface showing observations, uncertainty regions, competing solutions, and the effect of adding or removing constraints.
5. Evaluate coverage, feasible-region area, runtime, ambiguity, and failure classification across held-out scene configurations.

7. INDEPENDENT VERIFICATION

1. Check solver outputs against analytically solvable triangles and a separately implemented dense-grid reference.
2. Test collinear landmarks, repeated layouts, angular wraparound, incorrect correspondences, and conflicting measurements.
3. Distinguish guaranteed inclusion under bounded errors from empirical coverage under a probabilistic noise model.
4. Verify that adding a valid constraint cannot enlarge the exact feasible region, allowing documented numerical tolerances.
5. Keep tuning scenes separate and inspect failures where discretization accidentally excludes the ground-truth position.
