# PROJECT CEDAR: PROCUREMENT AND ASSURANCE FOR DUAL-USE TECHNOLOGY

## 1. Mission

Act as my lead software engineer, procurement analyst, and research collaborator.

Build a substantial, polished portfolio project that I can demonstrate to defense contractors, systems integrators, and technology suppliers, including prospective audiences such as INKAS or Marconi.

The project must demonstrate that I can connect:

- Procurement requirements with verifiable supplier evidence.
- Software security findings with their actual relevance to a purchasing decision.
- Technical uncertainty with clear commercial consequences.
- Policy requirements with enforceable software behavior.
- Mathematical analysis with practical decision support.
- AI-assisted development with disciplined verification and honest reporting.

My name is Noah Weinberger. My relevant background includes AI red teaming, policy analysis, data provenance, and an interest in defense technology and formal methods. Use that context to shape the portfolio, but only attribute work, credentials, professional relationships, and results to me when supported by information actually provided.

### The product

Create **CEDAR**, a locally runnable procurement and assurance workbench for comparing unarmed civilian technology packages.

Its central question is:

> Given these requirements, supplier documents, software inventories, costs, and unresolved questions, which configurations can we defensibly consider, what supports that conclusion, and what changes would invalidate it?

The flagship demonstration will compare fictional UAV inspection packages for a civilian organization. Each package can include an aircraft, ground station, software, batteries, training, maintenance, and support.

The software must help a buyer trace a recommendation back to its evidence, understand uncertainty, and preserve the reasoning when documents or requirements change.

Build an application that supports a coherent purchasing workflow from document ingestion to a reviewable decision package. Give the project enough technical substance that an engineer can inspect the implementation and enough commercial clarity that a procurement manager can understand its value.

This is an execution mission. Perform the work through implementation, verification, documentation, and presentation materials. Do not stop after proposing an architecture.

## 4. Define the commercial problem before building

Write a concise problem statement from the perspective of three people:

1. A procurement manager comparing technically different offers.
2. An engineering reviewer checking whether the evidence supports required capabilities.
3. A program manager trying to understand unresolved risks, cost exposure, and the consequences of a supplier amendment.

Explain the decisions each person needs to make and the evidence they need.

Translate those needs into a narrow product promise:

> CEDAR organizes the evidence behind a procurement comparison, identifies unresolved requirements, makes assumptions visible, and preserves a reviewable decision history.

Identify where the prototype could create value, such as reducing repeated document searches, making comparison rules consistent, exposing hidden assumptions, and simplifying amendment reviews.

Treat those as proposed benefits until measured. Do not invent time savings, financial returns, customer demand, or operational impact.

## 7. Implement the evidence and requirements model

Make the evidence model a central engineering contribution.

Every requirement must include:

- A stable identifier.
- Its exact wording.
- Its category and version.
- Whether it is mandatory, scored, or informational.
- Applicable configurations.
- The acceptable type of evidence.
- Units and comparison rules where relevant.
- Any effective date or expiration condition.
- The source or stated reason for the requirement.

Every extracted assertion must include:

- The supplier and configuration it concerns.
- The original document identifier and content hash.
- A page, section, table cell, or other inspectable location.
- The original passage or value.
- The normalized value, if one is used.
- The transformation applied.
- Its extraction and review status.
- Its applicable date and document version.
- Any unresolved interpretation.

Keep these distinctions explicit:

- A supplier made a claim.
- The supplied evidence satisfies the buyer’s stated documentary requirement.
- An independent test established a capability.
- The actual product will perform that way in the field.

The prototype must not collapse those into one status.

### Requirement assessment states

Use a small, documented state model such as:

- Supported by the required evidence.
- Not satisfied.
- Unresolved.
- Conflicting.
- Stale.
- Not applicable, with an explicit reason.

Define precedence rules when multiple observations apply.

An unresolved mandatory requirement must remain visible and prevent an unconditional eligibility recommendation. Missing evidence must not silently become a pass, a zero, or a factual accusation.

Separate eligibility from preference. A high score must not compensate for an unmet mandatory requirement.

## 8. Build the procurement analysis

### Cost model

Calculate a transparent multiyear cost comparison for the six-unit fleet.

Include relevant categories supported by the fictional documents:

- Initial equipment.
- Shared ground equipment.
- Required software.
- Training.
- Scheduled support.
- Consumables and replacement assumptions.
- Optional items.
- Explicit escalation clauses.
- Costs that remain unspecified.

Keep quoted values separate from illustrative planning assumptions.

Use one declared reporting currency. If conversion is included, record the rate, date, source, and interpretation. Synthetic exchange rates must be labeled as illustrative.

Handle taxes, discounting, and inflation only through explicit assumptions. Preserve an undiscounted baseline.

Do not invent an expected value for every unknown cost. Where evidence supports only a range or open exposure, display that limitation.

### Scoring and sensitivity

Implement transparent scoring formulas for the fourteen scored requirements.

Provide three clearly labeled preference profiles representing different fictional buyer priorities. Keep mandatory requirements consistent unless a separate requirement version explicitly changes them.

Show:

- The score contribution of every criterion.
- The weights and normalization.
- The treatment of missing scored evidence.
- Whether a ranking depends on uncertain values.
- Which plausible weight changes alter the result.
- Whether any configuration is dominated under the declared comparison.

Where scored evidence is unresolved, consider justified score intervals instead of replacing uncertainty with a single invented number.

If you sample weights or uncertain parameters, explain what the sampling distribution means. A frequency of winning in an arbitrary parameter sweep is not automatically a probability of being the best real-world purchase.

Include independently checked small examples and limiting cases.

### Clarification queue

Generate a prioritized list of questions a buyer would need answered before proceeding.

Each question must identify:

- The relevant supplier and configuration.
- The requirement or cost exposure.
- The missing or conflicting evidence.
- Why the answer could affect eligibility, ranking, or cost.
- The exact supporting source locations.

Draft these questions inside the application and export package. Do not send them.

## 10. Preserve decisions through amendments and approvals

Implement versioned decision snapshots.

A snapshot must bind together:

- The requirements version.
- Source-document versions.
- Extracted assertions and review decisions.
- Cost assumptions.
- Scoring policy.
- Applicable dates.
- Unresolved questions.
- The recommendation and its limitations.

Create distinct demonstration roles for:

- Analyst.
- Technical reviewer.
- Approver.

Enforce relevant permissions on the server. A disabled interface button is insufficient.

The AI component, if present, may suggest extractions, explanations, or clarification questions. It must not grant itself reviewer authority or approve a decision.

### Amendment behavior

When a document or requirement changes:

1. Preserve the previous version.
2. Identify affected assertions and assessments.
3. Recompute the relevant results.
4. Explain what changed and why.
5. Mark affected approvals as stale.
6. Require a deliberate review action before representing a new snapshot as approved.

Handle concurrent amendments and approval attempts consistently.

### Export integrity

Produce a portable decision package with:

- The report.
- A structured decision record.
- Evidence references.
- Input and output hashes.
- Version information.
- A verification command.

If signing is implemented, use a maintained cryptographic library and clearly identified demonstration keys.

Explain the trust assumptions. A signature can bind an artifact to a key under stated assumptions; it does not establish that a supplier’s underlying claim is true.

## 12. Build a professional interface

Use a restrained interface suitable for an engineering or procurement meeting.

Prioritize these views:

1. **Procurement overview:** scope, eligible configurations, unresolved gates, and cost ranges.
2. **Requirements matrix:** configurations against requirements, with understandable states.
3. **Evidence viewer:** direct access to the source behind each material assertion.
4. **Comparison view:** costs, scores, assumptions, and sensitivity.
5. **Software assurance view:** component findings and their procurement relevance.
6. **Change review:** amendments and their effects on previous conclusions.
7. **Decision package:** current snapshot, review status, and export.

Make important conclusions inspectable within one or two deliberate actions.

Show uncertainty through precise labels. Avoid converting every issue into a red warning or hiding difficult questions behind a generic confidence percentage.

Use readable typography, keyboard navigation, sensible empty states, and useful error messages.

Create screenshots from the implemented application. Any design mockup must be labeled separately.

## 14. Verification and evaluation

Build verification around consequential failure modes.

### Independent calculations

Check representative examples using separate calculations for:

- Unit normalization.
- Package quantities.
- Multiyear cost totals.
- Score normalization.
- Missing-value treatment.
- Ranking changes.
- Dominance or bound calculations.

### Eligibility and evidence checks

Demonstrate that:

- Every unconditional eligibility recommendation has the required supporting evidence.
- An unsupported mandatory requirement cannot pass through a high aggregate score.
- A supplier declaration cannot substitute for an independent test when the requirement demands the latter.
- Contradictory and stale evidence remain visible.
- Every material citation resolves to the recorded source version.
- Reordering documents does not change an otherwise equivalent result.

### State and permission checks

Demonstrate that:

- An analyst cannot perform an approver-only action.
- A changed evidence snapshot cannot silently inherit approval.
- Concurrent updates do not create an apparently current approval over inconsistent inputs.
- Exported records reproduce the decision being displayed.

### Benchmark reporting

Report extraction quality, assessment errors, unsupported citations, unresolved cases, runtime, and resource use where meaningful.

Keep denominators explicit.

Separate:

- Performance on clean synthetic fixtures.
- Performance on varied or held-out synthetic layouts.
- Performance on any permitted real documents.
- Results of actual user review, if any.

Do not claim real-world accuracy, user time savings, or enterprise readiness from a synthetic evaluation alone.

Once the defined risks are sufficiently checked, stop expanding tests merely to increase their count.

### Optional formal-methods extension

After the core application is complete, consider a small formal model of the review and amendment state machine.

A useful property would be:

> Every valid current approval refers to the current evidence and policy versions, and every required approval condition holds for that snapshot.

If a Lean proof is attempted, use an actual pinned toolchain, report the theorem and assumptions, and show a successful kernel check without unfinished obligations or newly assumed project conclusions.

Keep the formal model’s scope separate from the web application, database, cryptographic assumptions, and physical-world evidence.

An unfinished proof remains an optional unfinished extension. It must not be described as a verified product.
