# Timing and interchange contract

All data are assistant-generated synthetic administrative inputs. No clinical interpretation is made.

Scenario JSON fields: `id`, `seed`, `horizon` (positive integer slots), `slot_minutes` (positive integer), `load_time` (positive integer slots), `handoff_time` and `turnaround_time` (nonnegative integer slots), `charge_duration` (positive integer slots), `vehicles` (list of `{id, unavailable: [[start,end],...]}`), `battery_ids` (unique strings, initially full), `charger_ids` (unique strings), `technician_ids` (unique strings), `travel` (nested integer matrix including `Depot` and each region), `requests` (list below), `weights` (nonnegative numbers named `rejection`, `lateness`, `charge`, `fairness`). Empty resource lists are valid stress cases. Vehicles, batteries and chargers are interchangeable within each type. Technicians supervise a whole charge and handle loading; travel needs no technician.

Request fields: `id`, `release`, `deadline` (nonnegative integer slots), `region`, `category`, `priority` (positive integer administrative multiplier), `cancelled` (boolean known when the request is revealed). Release is the earliest loading start. Deadline is a soft target for package handoff, not return. Cancellation is pre-dispatch administrative cancellation supplied at arrival; no dynamic mid-service cancellation is modeled. Each request uses one vehicle and one full battery, which becomes depleted. Service is indivisible. Battery energy is binary, with one full charge per mission, independent of trip duration.

For loading start s, delivery = s + load_time + travel[Depot][region] + handoff_time. Resource return e = delivery + travel[region][Depot] + turnaround_time. Vehicle and battery are occupied on [s,e); battery becomes depleted at e. Loading occupies one technician on [s,s+load_time). Charging occupies one depleted battery, one charger and one technician on [c,c+charge_duration), then produces a full battery. All intervals are half open. Releases and charge completions at t precede acquisitions at t. All operations finish by horizon. Batteries may remain depleted at horizon; no mandatory terminal recharge.

Vehicle unavailability forbids overlap of the complete vehicle interval, including loading and turnaround. The availability calendar is known to every policy at time zero. No paths or real weather are modeled. The directed travel matrix need not be symmetric or metric: trips always start and end at Depot, so inter-region entries do not affect scheduling. Zero off-diagonal travel is allowed only in small abstract reference fixtures.

Schedule JSON: `scenario_id`, `policy`, `information` (`offline` or `online`), `services` (list of `{request_id, vehicle_id, battery_id, technician_id, start, delivery, end}`), `charges` (list of `{battery_id, charger_id, technician_id, start, end}`), `unserved` (list of `{request_id, status, reason}`, status `cancelled` or `rejected`), `solver` (metadata). Every request is in exactly one service or unserved record. Online service starts must be at or after release. An online policy receives only requests with release <= current time; its planning forecasts contain no future requests. Exported decisions will record visible IDs and executed starts. Previous service and charge starts are irreversible.

Objective: rejection_weight * sum(priority of rejected noncancelled requests) + lateness_weight * sum(priority * late minutes of served requests) + charge_weight * number of charges + fairness_weight * maximum regional rejection fraction. Cancelled requests are excluded from all service-rate denominators. Empty regions are omitted. Fairness is regional, unweighted by priority. Category distributions are also reported. Objective weights are illustrative cost units, not clinical values. Charging queues measure elapsed time from depleted return until charge starts; batteries never recharged are right-censored and reported separately.

Hand fixture: two vehicles, two batteries, one charger, two technicians, horizon 8, one minute per slot, loading 1, outbound travel 1, other durations 0, charging 2. Four simultaneous requests with deadlines 2,2,6,8 and priority 1. Serve first two on [0,2), charge B1 [2,4), B2 [4,6), serve R3 [4,6), R4 [6,8). There is a charging conflict at t=2: both batteries need the sole charger. The objective is 2 with charge_weight=1, rejection_weight=200, lateness_weight=1, fairness_weight=0. Any all-served solution needs at least two recharges, and any rejection costs at least 200, so 2 is the global minimum for this fixture.
