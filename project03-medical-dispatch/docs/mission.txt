6. IMPLEMENTATION SEQUENCE

1. Define the request, vehicle, battery, charger, technician, and availability schemas with coherent units, explicit turnaround times, and a documented interpretation of every deadline and priority.
2. Generate reproducible travel-time matrices and demand fixtures, including a few analytically solvable cases. State whether symmetry and triangle inequalities are assumed or irrelevant.
3. Implement a discrete-event simulator with explicit resource acquisition and release. Define deterministic tie breaking and policies for cancellations, unavailable vehicles, and infeasible requests.
4. Implement two transparent heuristics and one exact or bounded solver formulation. Prevent future information from leaking into online policies and distinguish offline comparisons clearly.
5. Run controlled sensitivity experiments for demand, charging capacity, weather availability, and objective weights. Preserve infeasible cases and explain their causes rather than dropping them.

7. INDEPENDENT VERIFICATION

1. Write an independent schedule checker that verifies request service, temporal precedence, battery accounting, vehicle overlap, charger capacity, and staff constraints without using solver internals.
2. Enumerate all feasible assignments for tiny cases and compare their best objective with the optimizer. Verify solver status, time limits, and optimality gaps honestly.
3. Test boundary cases including zero requests, simultaneous deadlines, no feasible vehicle, charger bottlenecks, cancelled requests, and a delivery that cannot complete within the declared horizon.
4. Compare policies using identical demand and disruption realizations, and separate clairvoyant offline optimization from online decisions based only on information available at that time.
5. Check objective sensitivity and service distribution so an aggregate improvement cannot conceal a large deterioration for one fictional region or request category.
